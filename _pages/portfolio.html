--- layout: archive title: "Research" permalink: /portfolio/ author_profile: true --- ## Current Work ### Doctoral Thesis: ### "Beyond the Edge: Can Mountain Huckleberry Establish in Newly Suitable Alpine Habitat?" As the climate warms, many plants are expected to move upslope, but will they actually find places to grow? My thesis tests this with mountain huckleberry (*Vaccinium membranaceum*), a shrub of major ecological and cultural importance in the Pacific Northwest. In Garibaldi Provincial Park, I planted 756 seedlings across 191 blocks spanning the species' core range, its upper edge, and alpine habitat beyond it. By tracking microclimate, soil nutrients, and microtopography (mapped with drone photogrammetry), I'm asking which small-scale conditions allow establishment, and whether models based on coarse climate averages overestimate how species will fare. This work is supervised by Drs. Courtney Collins and Amy Angert and exists within the broader research initiative: Alpine Horizons. ![Mountain huckleberry seedling transplanted in Garibaldi Provincial Park]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/vacmem_seedling.jpg){: style="width: 600px; max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0 8px;"} Mountain huckleberry seedling transplanted in Garibaldi Provincial Park (2026). ## Past Work ### U.S. Geological Survey: Research Technician
